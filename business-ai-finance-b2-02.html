CASE STUDY · NORTHBRIDGE RETAIL

An AI system predicted a cash shortage. Was it right?

Northbridge Retail, a medium-sized European company, had experienced steady revenue growth for three years. However, despite increasing sales, the company regularly struggled with cash flow.

Its finance department introduced an AI-powered forecasting system. The software analysed historical sales, supplier payments, seasonal trends and customer payment behaviour.

Within two weeks, the system identified a potential liquidity problem. It predicted that the company's available cash could fall below €100,000 within the next 60 days. Surprisingly, the traditional financial forecast had shown no serious problems.

The AI model highlighted three early warning signs: several large customers were paying invoices later than usual, inventory costs had increased, and sales growth was slowing in one important market.

The finance director, Emma Collins, was initially sceptical. She believed the system might be overreacting to temporary changes. Rather than accepting the prediction immediately, her team investigated the data. They discovered that one major customer was experiencing financial difficulties and had delayed several payments.

Northbridge negotiated new payment terms with suppliers, postponed a non-essential investment and contacted customers with overdue invoices. Six weeks later, the company avoided the predicted cash shortage.

However, the finance director emphasised an important distinction: the software had identified a risk, but human judgement was responsible for evaluating the evidence and choosing how to respond. The company continued using AI for forecasting, but all major financial decisions still required human approval.
