    The Hidden Cost of Convenience

    ◉

    1. Let’s talk

    Think of an app or service you regularly use to save time.

    1. When did you last pay extra to make something easier? Was it worth it?
    2. What small inconvenience would you accept to save money or help someone else?
    3. When a service saves your time, who might be doing the extra work?

    I used to…
    The biggest advantage for me is…
    It’s worth it when…

    2. Match the expressions

    Match the eight expressions with their definitions. Pick two and give a real example for each.

    3. Read and infer

    Read about a building that changed the way it receives deliveries. Then choose the best answers.

    The time we save, and the work we pass on

    When Maya moved into her new apartment, she used to get nearly everything delivered to her door. The extra charge seemed reasonable. Then one week she paid for express delivery and still spent twenty minutes helping three different couriers find the entrance. On Friday, her neighbour had to leave a meeting to take in a parcel for her. Maya began to see a hidden cost that her delivery app never showed.

    She suggested a one-month trial to the building’s residents. Instead of having every parcel delivered to their doors, they would send routine orders to a collection point near the station. At first she thought she would have to make a special trip every time. But she started picking up her parcels on the way home, and within two weeks she had got used to it. The upfront cost was zero, and she stopped paying for several express deliveries a month.

    For other residents, the trade-off looked different. The collection point closed before one nurse finished her shift, and an older resident found the walk difficult. Both had been used to receiving parcels at the door. A single rule would have shifted the burden from couriers to people who had few other options.

    The residents kept the collection point as the default for routine orders and left doorstep delivery available to anyone who wanted it. They also put clearer delivery directions in the building app. Maya still used doorstep delivery on busy weeks. What had changed was that she could say who was saving time, who was doing the extra work, and when the convenience was worth paying for.

    4. Talk about changing habits

    used to + infinitive · a past habit that has changed. I used to order every meal online.

    be used to + noun or -ing · something feels normal. I’m used to cooking after work.

    get used to + noun or -ing · something gradually starts to feel normal. I got used to shopping on Sundays.

    Complete the sentences. Then tell your teacher one thing you are getting used to.

    ▥

    5. Choose a delivery policy

    Your building has 30 flats. Delivery drivers often wait outside, some residents work until late, and the nearest collection point closes at 7 p.m.

    1. For each option, who gains time and who takes on more work?
    2. What condition would make your preferred policy fairer?

    Choose a policy to see a question to discuss.

    6. Negotiate with a neighbour

    Agree on a one-month trial and decide how you’ll tell whether it worked.

    Resident A

    You work from home. Couriers regularly ring your bell for other residents. You want fewer interruptions and clearer instructions.

    Resident B

    You work late and sometimes collect heavy orders for your parent. You need a dependable way to get them delivered.

    I can see why you’d prefer that.
    Would you be willing to try…?
    That would work for me as long as…

    7. Give a 60-second opinion

    “If a service saves me time, the price is all I need to consider.” Do you agree?

    Give a real example, explain a trade-off, and acknowledge one reason someone might disagree. Use at least two expressions from the lesson.

    Write a 140–180 word message suggesting a small change in your building, workplace or community. Explain the problem, a hidden cost, and how you would run a fair trial. Use at least three expressions from the lesson and one form of used to.
